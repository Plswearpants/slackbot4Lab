## Abstract

Dong Chen is a senior member of the group whose current work centers on the four-probe/miniSMP hardware line (connector fabrication, jig assembly, and the pre-amp rack build with Markus) and on developing MC-SBD (multi-channel sparse blind deconvolution), a quasiparticle-interference reconstruction method they built from the ground up. Earlier in their time in the lab they were the de facto operator and troubleshooter of the group's Createc and Tesla STM systems, handling everything from pump failures and vacuum leaks to tip preparation, wiring repairs, and bakeouts. Their principal sample systems have been PtSn4/AuSn4 and ZrSiTe, where they combine STM/QPI data with DFT and tight-binding calculations to characterize defect populations and surface-state scattering; this work has led into first-author writing on defect-specific QPI and MC-SBD methodology papers. They are expert in QPI data analysis and defect statistics, sparse deconvolution algorithm design, and practical STM instrumentation and hardware fabrication.

### 2025

Dong Chen spent 2025 developing and applying a multi-kernel sparse blind deconvolution (MC-SBD) method for disentangling QPI signals from overlapping defect types in STM data, alongside hands-on work on the lab's Createc STM system. On the hardware side, he ran motor tests, installed the big turbo pump and an empty CF flange, and coordinated recommissioning of a small turbo pump and the compressor/gate-valve system ahead of pump-down and leak testing, working toward test scans on Cu(111). The bulk of his year was algorithmic: he redefined the demixing-score metric (using 1−IoU between activations), built a synthetic-data phase-space benchmark spanning defect density, kernel/observation area ratio, and SNR, and diagnosed subtleties like flipped-kernel artifacts and the tradeoffs between random versus selected kernel initialization. He corrected a sign error traced to a typo in the underlying dictionary-learning paper, fixed a bug in multi-defect scattering simulations, and implemented tunable quasiparticle lifetimes via the electron-electron interaction term in the Green's function, discussing quasiparticle coherence and QPI decay with Sarah in light of Jennifer's paper. He rebuilt the observation-generation pipeline (thresholded kernel boundaries, lattice-unit activations upsampled to pixels), reworked the optimization to run on full 3D energy-slice blocks with a shared activation map, and added local (mini-iteration) residual updates and lambda-scaling corrections for multi-slice runs. Much of the spring and summer involved applying the algorithm to real datasets — LaSbTe, ZrSiTe, LiFeAs, and Brandon's AuSn4/PtSn4-adjacent data — building a preprocessing pipeline (streak removal via Laplacian-based detection and interpolation, Bragg-peak handling, defect masking) after debugging discrepancies with Brandon's original OSF code. Notable results included successfully disentangling two opposing kernels in LaSbTe, isolating three dominant defects in ZrSiTe with high fidelity (variance ratios ~1.05–1.3) across an 80-slice block spanning 160–800 meV, identifying kernel splitting by defect orientation, and probing possible floating-band QPI signatures with Sarah near 197 meV. He also contributed defect-state analysis on Pt/Sn defects and AuSn4 for a manuscript rebuttal to Nature Communications reviewers, and searched for QPI reference materials with non-square lattices per Sarah's suggestions (graphene, TMDs, kagome metals).
