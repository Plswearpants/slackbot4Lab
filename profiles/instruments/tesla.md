## Abstract

Tesla is a Joule-Thomson STM/AFM with integrated ARPES, commissioned in 2019 and housed at 61 Brimacombe. It operates at 1 K with a 170-hour LHe holding time, enabling undisturbed long measurements such as quasiparticle interference studies extending up to one week at improved resolution. A dry superconducting split-pair 1D magnet provides vertical fields up to 3 Tesla.

Both standard STM tips and QPlus AFM tips work on the JT platform, enabling spin-polarised STM and magnetic force microscopy when combined with the field. A bidirectional transfer chamber connects the JT STM/AFM to an ARPES system, permitting analysis of the very same sample by complementary techniques; the ARPES side is optimised for quick high-resolution photoemission and has a cooling/heating stage spanning -130 to 830 degrees C.

## Systems

LaSbTe, CaPt, PtIr, NbSe2, GdSbTe, SiC, PtAs, FeAs, HCl, PIC, PGYP, EuSb2

### 2026-05

Throughout May the team chased a persistent RF-noise problem degrading the Tesla's energy resolution: Jisun tested BLP-1.9+ filters at various points (finding that placing one before the tunneling-current preamp caused oscillator-like saturation), systematically disconnected TCC, AFM preamp, and PIC coarse-motor lines to isolate the noise source, and traced a 48 MHz bias artifact to HVS4/PIC wiring. After extensive sleuthing on filter part numbers (Omicron TCC/PIC filters, Tusonix 4209-053, Spectrum Control) and vendor quotes, Jisun settled on ordering five 250 V D-sub RF filters from RFImmunity (~$1000 + $250 shipping) rather than Omicron's ~$6500 set; meanwhile the LiFeAs samples proved too degraded for use, and by month's end Thomas Neal prepped and began mounting LaSbTe crystals (batches 3a52c/3a72c) as the next sample to try.

### 2026-03

This month Tesla struggled to resolve the superconducting gap on NbSe2 despite stable junctions down to 5 mV: Jisun added a home-built low-pass filter after the AKOM bias output and performed capacitive compensation tuning of the preamp (300 Hz, 300 mV modulation), cutting cross-talk on the tunneling line from ~75 mV/12 pA down to ~10 mV/1.2 pA, but the gap remained absent and tip changes (up to 1 nA) caused erratic spectra suggesting a compromised 0.25 mm PtIr tip. With JT/SPM temperatures creeping back up to ~2.2 K and unable to hold 1 K, the cryostat was deliberately warmed starting ~March 20 (head retracted/locked, 1 K pot emptied), and on March 25 Sarah switched to pumping both sides of the JT line, dropping the JT pressure significantly per Jiabin/Sarah's monitoring.

### 2026

Over the first two months of 2026, the Tesla STM went through a long, frustrating diagnostic campaign centered on a missing superconducting gap. Jisun continued weekly cryogen fills and field-emission tip conditioning, dealing with multi-ended tips after landing on rough areas of both left and right NbIrTe4/LaSbTe crystals in early January; a suspected partial JT plug caused the head to heat to ~4.6 K after FE and cool anomalously slowly, but by January 29 the plug seemed to clear on its own once JT and SPM temperatures dropped back below 1 K. After ruling out a temperature-dependent effect on the doped crystals (identical spectra at 4.5 K and 1 K, no E_F peak either termination), the group swapped samples—removing orth-LaSbTe and mounting a thin NbSe2 crystal grown by Mohamed on January 31—and obtained good atomic resolution with a ~3x CDW modulation but no sign of the ~2.5 mV SC gap. This triggered weeks of electronics troubleshooting: Jisun tee'd the SC5/AKOM bias output into AI6 to check zero-bias offsets, found a persistent ~1.8-1.9 mV system-level offset separate from the SC5 range-dependent offset (-4 mV in 10V range, -0.4 mV in 1V range), and quantified bias noise at roughly ±4 µV via slope-subtracted residuals. A stray discovery on Feb 20 showed background noise dropping to an unprecedented ~100 aA baseline (letting a scan run with proportional gain 500 fm / integral gain 6 nm/s instead of the usual 5 pm/30 nm/s), but this was traced by Feb 25 to the AKOM's current-gain and bias-gain LEDs freezing in an unresponsive state, resolved by power-cycling the whole Nanonis stack; Jisun then worked with Thomas on grounding among the copper bar, temperature-sensor outlet, and Nanonis controller outlet to chase a 60 Hz peak and stray ground currents (0.1-several µA). Jisun also tried an alternate current input (AI2) and set up AO3 as an alternate bias channel via generic sweep tests, finding no improvement over the original bias line. New Linde-supplied gas regulators for Tesla and the 4-probe arrived Feb 24. By month's end the original NbSe2 crystal was removed intact for Mohamed to independently verify its superconductivity, and a second NbSe2 crystal was loaded into the load lock for cleaving, with the group still undecided between pursuing lock-in/bias-divider schemes or switching to a higher-Tc test material like LiFeAs or FeSe.

### 2024

In 2024 the Tesla STM underwent a lengthy head-repair campaign after Jisun and Jörn vented the chamber on January 23–24, pulled the insert, and removed the LN2 and LHe shields following the Tesla disassembly slide deck (reaching slide 14, with the 1K shield off by January 26); capacitance checks (Xc normal, Zc pinned near zero) localized the fault to the head itself, and by January 30 Jisun had removed the PIC, SCC, and TCC pin sets and the braid connecting the head to the JT, discovering that one wire had detached from a TCC pin. The fix stalled for weeks on the hunt for the discontinued AMP 549043-1 crimp tool—Mouser, Chem/Phys electronics, and Josh all came up empty—until Omicron (via Eike, with backchannel help from Amy and Matthias) shipped a "care package" (FedEx tracking 272202246467) that arrived March 21 containing wiring, pins, and a genuine manual crimp tool. Omicron's suggested fix proved physically inaccessible given the head's tight clearances, so Jisun requested 3D drawings and, on April 2–3, carried the head to building 463 to inspect the wiring under a high-resolution microscope, concluding the ceramic piece could not safely be removed and that all broken-wire failures were caused purely by thermal cycling, not motor motion—clarified further when manual actuation of the coarse motor revealed only the central prism moves in Z while the inner body shifts in X. Jörn and Jisun then worked with Timothy and Pinder to adapt the lab's spot welder (one electrode replaced with a cable/tweezer tip mimicking a jeweler's spot welder, with kapton-taped probes to prevent shorts) for an in-situ weld directly onto the inner body, a plan still being finalized at month's end. Separately, Jörn changed the oil in the Tesla air compressor on February 21, and on April 15 Jisun found that the head had been knocked over on the desk, revealing a pre-existing crack in an insulating piece and a bent rod nearby, which she straightened enough to permit reassembly of the mating part.

### 2023

Over the course of 2023 the Tesla STM in the scanning-probe lab underwent a full controller transition and extensive tip and calibration work led primarily by Jisun Ryu, with Jörn Bannies handling day-to-day LaSbTe scanning and field-emission tip conditioning. The long-awaited Nanonis SC5/OC5/RC5e controller stack, delayed in shipping since January, finally arrived by the front desk on Feb 3 and was installed and cabled to the Tesla chamber over Feb 16–17 (with help from Amy on the MistralGUI connection), leading to first atomic resolution with Nanonis on Feb 24 and, by March 22, high-resolution topography resolving both the square net and the Sb-chain "waffle/houndstooth" corrugation on LaSbTe. Along the way Jisun caught and corrected a calibration error, discovering the piezo scan range was actually ~16% larger in x/y and ~25% larger in z than the original installation values rather than smaller as previously assumed. Cryogen handling was tracked quantitatively, e.g. a Feb 16 fill using 14.4 L LHe (level 26→500 mm) with 6.02 L (160.2 CF) recovered. In parallel, Jisun designed a cold-cleave/tip-ebeam stage and vacuum-suitcase support frame for Tesla, standardizing on metric flanges (leveraging spare metric hardware from the original Tesla installation) while keeping some imperial screws (#6-32) for the tip/cleaving stage, and worked through material concerns with Josh regarding SS304/316 versus 18-8 stainless and K-type thermocouple magnetism near the magnet given Tesla's regular quench cycles. In March, after persistent tip trouble requiring repeated field emission treatments, an anomalous tunneling-current dropout during the first I-V sweep after feedback shutoff was diagnosed by oscilloscope-tee testing at the SC5 Z-output and at the AKOM/PRE cable junctions; the system was temporarily switched back to the Matrix controller to compare behavior, and borrowing the LT system's AKOM was proposed as a further diagnostic step.

### 2022

The Tesla STM spent most of 2022 fighting its own noise floor while riding out lab logistics and a growing LaSbTe project. In January, Jisun Kim ran systematic noise measurements on Au/mica with the block floated and deflated, isolating a persistent 1.3 Hz peak that could be suppressed by tip conditioning, and cross-checked it against accelerometer data with Sarah Ryan, confirming a 0.85 Hz isolator resonance that vanished when the block was unfloated. Cryogen handling got harder after a new LN2 dewar was installed, pushing fills past an hour each; a lingering LN2 crate shipping/customs issue with Omicron (contacts Beaton/Brandon/Eike) dragged on unresolved through mid-January. The block was fully floated and Tesla realigned on 1/17, with a fresh Au/mica sample cleaned, annealed, and loaded, and Jörn Bannies' LaSbTe crystal mounted after being pulled from the furnace and cleaved (only half the sample cleaved cleanly, but the auto-approach still landed on it). Through late January and February, broadband 45/55/75/95 Hz noise plagued scans depending on tip position on the LaSbTe sample/epoxy boundary, eventually resolved by moving back near the original landing spot; a stray 60 Hz noise also appeared and was tracked down after cryogen fills. Bias-dependent topos from -1V to +1V (0.2V steps) on LaSbTe revealed a real, reproducible short-range "plaid"/patchy background pattern later confirmed on larger scan windows with near-atomic resolution, generating significant excitement about a possible QPI/orthorhombic-distortion signature, though an attempted grid map on this feature was lost in late February when the tip degraded overnight (likely due to a field-emission event) after being left withdrawn since Friday. In parallel, Jisun and James tuned the CE/tension-plate connections and calibrated capacitance values (work that also fed into the neighboring Beast system), and a new WFM pattern/dimension was finalized for compatibility with both Tesla and Beast sample plates. Jisun also modified the sample-plate wobble-stick jaw with an epoxied rod in preparation for a custom vacuum-suite flange test with Bruce. By month's end, with Jörn shifting time back to EuSb2 transport measurements (PPMS troubleshooting) and crystal growth for upcoming beamtime, Jisun took over continued STM data collection on the patchy LaSbTe crystal to keep the project moving.

### 2021

The MFLI/Omicron controller issue prompted plans by Amy and Jisun to handle a repair themselves or coordinate with Eike if he visited, while the compressed-air infrastructure for the vault got reworked in early January: Bobby installed a new desiccant system in the 47 service corridor, separated the Tesla-side line from the main 47 compressor, and Jisun ordered a backup teal air compressor (CAD 637.28) alongside the existing red backup; the vault B block, left disconnected over the holidays, was found sitting at 68 psi and was vented, then deliberately fully deflated (dropping from ~35 psi to 0) after Sarah raised concerns about piston "walking" and a repeat of a previous dramatic "thump" when the block was last set down, and noise spectra were taken in both floated and deflated states, ultimately showing the 1.32 Hz peak (3.14 pm vs. 2.59 pm) was intrinsic to the microscope rather than block-related, with additional block resonances located near 0.85 Hz and 2.4 Hz. Separately, Amy chased a recurring Fermi-edge grounding mystery (edge appearing above the expected 16.8 eV) that resolved itself without a clear explanation despite thermocouple-grounding experiments. Jisun brought up a new PtAs sample with Ca clusters, first achieving atomic resolution (with a 17% piezo calibration correction) and then, working at 1 K, resolving a superconducting-gap-like feature with coherence-peak structure, measured via single-point spectroscopy at 200 pA setpoint with T-raster times of 3.11 ms and 11.43 ms; the extracted gap size was about 9.8 meV against a bulk Tc of ~31 K, though a persistent zero-bias offset and high, non-zero zero-bias conductance led Doug and Sarah to question whether this reflected an anisotropic/multiband gap or lingering tip/instrument artifacts reminiscent of early LiFeAs data, and Jisun began checking the offset with an oscilloscope while Sarah proposed adding a known superconductor standard to the Tesla for future temperature/offset calibration. On the infrastructure side, a building elevator outage during this period nearly stranded LHe transport, with LHe levels tracked down to 69 mm and then 17 mm before a refill restored the bath, and Jisun managed to keep LN2 filling on schedule thanks to James pre-positioning a dewar; this incident reopened discussion of installing a basement recovery scale and pre-staging a dewar to avoid future disruptions like the one that had affected Seokhwan's runs over the holidays. On the ARPES/graphene side, Amy's continuing SiC/graphene work culminated in recognizing that the long-mysterious STM periodicity was in fact the true 6√3×6√3 R30 graphene/SiC superstructure rather than any lithium-intercalation feature, resolving months of "weird island" and LEED-pattern confusion despite inconclusive attempts to characterize residual sodium/lithium contamination via TOF-SIMS, XPS, or EDX at outside facilities (4D Labs/CLS).

### 2020

In 2020 the Tesla JT-STM went through a long sequence of repairs and setbacks. The year opened with Amy re-securing an epoxy/solder joint at an outer corner of some hardware, followed by a full bake cycle: degassing and ion-pump start on January 8–9 (pressure 2e-9 mbar dropping to 6e-9 mbar, ARPES turbo drawing ~1 A, transfer turbo 0.1 A), then an ARPES bake begun January 18 and finished around noon on the 19th. Just before that cooldown, Amy discovered the z coarse motor gave no capacitance signal (x was fine), a fault Jisun and Sarah suspected was heat/bake-related and possibly warranty-covered; the QPlus sensor itself survived reinstallation. In February, with the crane booked through Giorgio, the cryostat was pulled, shields reinstalled, piezo capacitances and temperature sensors rechecked, and Jisun added a finer mesh over the chamber's coarse bottom grid to enable cleaving, sacrificing some pumping speed; the system was rebaked (bake finished Feb 19) and began cooling with nitrogen and then helium, with Mag1/2 reaching ~5 K and JT/SPM ~25–31 K by Feb 26, alongside a software install by Hongyun on the Tesla and ARPES control computers. Separately, the ARPES turbo pump was diagnosed by Pfeiffer as likely leaking near the pump (~1 A draw at 5e-9 mbar vs. an expected 0.2–0.3 A) and was shipped back, a pre-sign-off issue that triggered a dispute over shipping/customs costs. In early March a bias-offset fault (measured values consistently 0.5 V off from set values) was traced to the preamp; Omicron's own qPlus preamp was briefly borrowed (correct voltages and tip-crash current confirmed) but its 9 m cable made routing through the pod impractical, so it was returned and the Tesla's own unit sent to Omicron in Germany for repair.
