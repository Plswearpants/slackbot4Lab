## Abstract

The Createc is a 4-Kelvin Scanning Tunnelling Microscope manufactured by the company of the same name, used for atomic imaging in the group's work on material structures and behaviours.

It is composed of two primary sections, a preparation chamber and an STM chamber, both UHV to decrease the rate of sample degradation. The preparation chamber is fitted with a suite of surface science preparation tools: an Ar+ sputtering gun, a low-energy electron diffraction setup, a quadrupole mass spectrometer, and an electron beam tip heating tool for tip annealing.

The STM chamber houses the STM head, fashioned on the Besocke-type STM-AFM head model. Its 3-legged design confers some thermal drift resistance, useful during long-term spectroscopy measurements, though the design limits the Z-axis motion range. A manipulating arm transfers sample crystals and STM tips between the two chambers.

## Systems

### 2024

The Createc STM spent early 2024 sidelined by a persistent short in the coarse piezo wiring, with Dong Chen leading the diagnostic and repair effort throughout January, February, and into March. Initial resistance-matrix and capacitance measurements across the coarse piezo in mid-January revealed cross-talk between wires, prompting plans to pull the Tesla head back out — a move complicated by the fact that the Createc insert also needed the shared crane, requiring coordination with Jisun and Giorgio over scheduling so as not to hold up Tesla's own repairs. By late February, systematic isolation testing (removing 27 unplugged pins, checking cryo/head grounds) narrowed the fault to the head wiring itself, and resistance tweaking pointed to a bundle in "tube 4," where E1, H1, I1, J1 and F2 were found shorting together. Attempts to separate F2 using spiral-cut Teflon sleeves (fabricated and tested off-head by Markus) were set aside in favor of Kapton-film dividers once the true short location was confirmed; by March 1–5 Dong Chen was insulating wires in tube 4, fashioning a smaller shielding tube, strapping wires with Teflon tape, and cutting the outer tube to install Kapton insulating layers, work that resolved most of the shorts except for one persistent J1–F2 contact still being addressed with a Kapton "booklet" separator. Separately, the cryostat's oxygen sensor was serviced in the Createc pod in late February, and around the same time the block lost float when its air compressor overheated and auto-shut-down (last serviced with an oil change two weeks prior); Dong Chen brought the compressor back online and restored block pressure toward the 95 psi target, subsequently running it at a reduced, just-floating pressure during the ongoing head repairs to ease compressor load. On the science side, QPI data collected on PtSn4 was analyzed throughout the period — of ten good-quality grids, five showed scattering patterns (two ripple-like, two dispersing rings) tied to adatom, crescent-defect, Pt-site, and croissant defect scattering — with Sarah flagging an early sharp feature as likely just a Bragg peak (confirmed via a VESTA/ImageJ FFT comparison), and Nic beginning DFT slab calculations (Sn and Pt vacancies) to help simulate defect STM images and connect Kz-dependent autocorrelation patterns to the measured dispersion.

### 2023

The Createc STM spent much of early 2023 idle after Dong Chen's AuSn4 cleave attempt was deferred and the system was warmed while he was away on leave (Jan 9–Feb 27, later extended by an ankle injury and visa delay into May); before shutting down, Markus and Dong collected overnight block-acceleration data and produced wavelet scalograms of the vibration isolation performance, noting an unexplained 10 Hz feature possibly tied to Nanonis controller noise. In late January the Createc's floating-block air compressor was temporarily commandeered by the "Beast" system while its own unit was repaired, and the isolation valve to the block was mistakenly left closed for 3–5 days (discovered by Jisun Feb 8), fully depressurizing the block's air isolators (0 psi) before a new compressor was installed for Beast and the Createc line reopened around Feb 10, feeding suspicions that the isolators run "too soft." At the end of March a more serious failure occurred: the scroll pump backing the Prep chamber's main turbo seized (overheating, stuck rotor, audible clicking/humming from a bad relay/bi-metal switch), and with the STM-Prep gate valve inadvertently left open, the turbo failure vented the whole system; Jisun and Markus diagnosed the fault, unplugged the hot scroll pump, closed the gate valve, and began repumping with the load-lock pump station, which itself threw F110/F119 overheating errors due to a broken fan-switch, requiring a box fan workaround and contact with VPC/Pfeiffer for repair guidance while a traveling turbo was considered as backup. A separate new turbo pump installed on the system was confirmed running fine, drawing slightly more power than before (12 W vs. the earlier 10–11 W). Additionally, a helium-recovery line miscabling was found linking Createc's dewar return to Omicron's, prompting Bobby to reconfigure the connections to avoid cross-contamination during LHe transfers. Scientifically, the instrument's main output this year was defect-statistics and QPI/CEC analysis of previously acquired PtSn4 topographic and spectroscopic data (led by Dong Chen with Sarah and Doug), including calibration of QPI Bragg-peak spacing to CEC Brillouin-zone dimensions (q = 2π√2/a), identification of 6 new Pt-site defect species in the low-RRR sample versus 2 in the high-RRR sample, and development of a binomial/Poisson counting-statistics framework (with Sarah, Jeff, and Doug) to properly quantify defect-density uncertainties across images of varying size.

### 2022

In early 2022 the Createc STM underwent a full chamber-opening and bakeout cycle managed largely by Dong Chen. A shipment of replacement gaskets from Kurt J. Lesker was delayed by supply-chain issues (originally due Jan 5, then pushed to Jan 12, arriving Jan 17), after which five gaskets were ordered under the CNZZ speed chart for a total of CAD 324.98. The chamber was closed and bolted on Jan 17, checked with a feeler gauge, and pumped down to the low-4 range overnight; a subsequent leak test on Jan 18 was confounded when the prep-chamber gauge (channel 2) gave a false reading because the valve between the big turbo and the prep chamber had not been opened, requiring a slow LL vent to equalize pressure before the filament (initially suspected burnt from degassing) was confirmed intact. A proper leak test on Jan 19 showed no leaks aside from the long-standing small leak at the manipulator-arm joint. Prep pressure hit a pre-bake record on Jan 24, and the bakeout box was assembled and bake started Jan 25 at 15:30, running for about a week with temperature monitored throughout; it finished Jan 31 with Prep and STM both at 1.50E-08 mbar, and a follow-up leak check on Feb 1 confirmed a leak-free, good vacuum. On Feb 4, Dong Chen ran a resistance test with a digital multimeter (no short found, though the high-voltage ohm-meter wasn't located) and noted the capacitance reading was small but stable; the same day, the crystal loaded in the manipulator arm—initially thought to be silver—was confirmed via logbook check to be Cu(111). Meanwhile, retrospective analysis of archived PtSn4 scans (by Dong Chen and Ashley) pushed back the earliest observed instances of the instrument's signature 4-fold symmetric rotating defect to Aug 31 and Aug 3, 2021, and turned up new dynamic defect behavior (a "croissant" defect flipping into a hole and back in PtSn4_Sep227.sxm, and "mushroom"-to-"butterfly" transitions at -320 mV) that the group began cataloguing for future reference alongside ongoing histogram-based work to determine the PtSn4 cleavage plane.

### 2021

Over the course of 2021 the createc STM went through an extended recovery from a manipulator/heat-shield failure that dominated much of the winter and spring: on Jan 4 the system warmed to 80 K after an elevator-related compressor issue forced Seokhwan to pull the sample and prep a new cleaving post, and a second compressor shutdown on Jan 18 (flagged by James) again threatened the floating Tesla block used for vibration isolation. Routine work continued in between—Seokhwan and Dong Chen loaded a freshly sputtered tip on Jan 15–16 and ran multi-day grid maps on the Rh 21% and Rh 18% iridate samples (bias sweeps from -800 mV to +800 mV), confirming three distinct surface terminations (Sr-O vs. topmost layers) by Jan 19, before Dong Chen prepped and transferred a PtSn4 sample in early February. On Feb 10 the heat shield jammed after the metal plate tilted (root cause: a loose screw connecting heat shield and plate), leaving the STM/cryostat stuck near 50 K and climbing with no manipulator-arm fix possible; this forced a vent and a full T-bar replacement, complicated by the old T-bar being asymmetrically bent and the first replacement part from Sven (Sentys) arriving with an incompatible tetragonal-body design that couldn't seat in the T-bar holder. A correctly designed T-bar was reordered, delayed by a US power outage, and finally arrived and tested in early March, including troubleshooting a persistent 5-degree tilt relative to the manipulator arm (resolved after re-seating) before Dong Chen ran leak checks on the STM head/cryo, the opened viewport, and manipulator arm (no leaks found, logged in the createc Labstep "leak test" protocol and RGA computer under 3_8_2021), clearing the way for a full bakeout starting March 12–13 (coordinated with the Beast's bake schedule) using half-power heaters monitored overnight for temperature uniformity.

### 2020

Throughout early 2020 the CreaTec low-temperature STM was run by Seokhwan Choi and Dong Chen on a sustained ZrSiTe defect-imaging campaign, cycling through repeated gridmaps on the "black hole" defect (46×46 nm², 470 px, runs of 48–68 hours, often at set biases of +1V) to hunt for a missing q-vector signature, with Brandon and Raquel cross-checking QPI simulations for Te and Zr defects against the data. Cryogen logistics were a constant thread — LHe fills (using dewars #4166 then #216, with fills such as 6.4 L bringing the head to 485 mm) and LN2 fills were run roughly twice weekly with help from Jisun, Ali, Rysa, and Jörn, and a real vacuum scare occurred on Jan 25 when Dong Chen found 3×10⁻¹⁰ Torr in the STM head after a fill, traced to a leaky recovery-line pipe connection that was subsequently fixed; a separate LN2 rubber-hose mistake on Feb 21 was noted as a cause of outer-chamber freezing. On March 16–18, in response to COVID-19 shutdowns, Seokhwan and Doug decided to end the ZrSiTe run, fully retracted the tip, let the STM head warm from base temperature (~20 K noted during warm-up) while keeping the system under vacuum with only the ion pump running, and closed the He recovery line (switched off by Doug on the 18th); Dong Chen also proposed a Windows 10 upgrade of the Nanonis control PC with IT support from Hongyun before departing for China on March 18–20.

### 2019

The Createc STM spent 2019 largely on ZrTe5 (Ti-doped) surface studies punctuated by a serious vacuum/pump crisis in March. Seokhwan opened the year imaging Ti-dopant contrast on ZrTe5, running iterative field-emission and tip-shaping treatments on the coin-gold reference (noting a persistent double-tip issue and surface contamination from Ca/O/H2O, leading to a standing recommendation to Ar-sputter and e-beam the gold prior to each cleave), while the oxygen sensor in the Createc pod was replaced (Jan 11). Through January and February, bias-dependent topographs and 2D dI/dV maps (−1 to +1 V) were collected on the Ti/Te defects, with tip crashes on the coin gold requiring repeated field-emission recovery and e-beaming; Pprep was pumped to 1.3×10⁻¹⁰ mbar after TSP on Jan 26. On March 5, Seokhwan found new terraces (topo2/topo3, 3–4 Å higher than the prior surface, Iset=50 pA, V=100 mV) and began terrace-resolved grid mapping, but a campus-wide power disruption on March 7 killed the tip immediately after grid maps finished on topo1 and topo3; the following day STM/cryo temperature readouts spiked to ~518 K, resolved by restarting the Nanonis software. Vacuum then degraded (Pstm ~1.2×10⁻⁹ mbar, suspected Ar-line and Ca-deposition-line leaks), the CO sensor began beeping intermittently (~120 ppm, cause inconclusive), and the big turbo pump overheated and suffered a hard overtemp failure; water- and fan-cooling let it run only at 37 kRPM (vs. 56 kRPM nominal) at ~40°C before failing again, and load-lock pressure was worse with it running (mid 10⁻⁵ vs mid 10⁻⁶ off) — Igor was brought in, Agilent quoted a CAD 3430 exchange, and a spare big turbo (traced to Mike Blades' retired lab) was tested and installed by Jisun and Brandon in mid-March. The replacement also underperformed, prompting a leak hunt focused on the 8"-to-6" reducer neck between turbo and gate valve, tightening of the turbo flange gap (300 µm → 200 µm), and a full system bakeout (thermocouples 100–110°C, final pressure 5×10⁻⁶ mbar, stopped March 27). By March 28–29 the tip was pulled, sputtered and e-beamed again in the prep chamber, with heat-shield opening causing pressure excursions to mid 10⁻⁸ mbar and a traveling turbo used on the gas line to reach 10⁻¹⁰ mbar, amid continued complaints about a loud, high-pitched turbo whir (12 W, 32°C) — closing out a year of routine LHe/LN2 fills (largely by Jisun, Brandon, Amy, and Giang) alongside the tip and vacuum maintenance.
