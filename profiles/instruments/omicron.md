# omicron

- kind: instrument
- channel: C3TR2MXQB
- source: https://lair.phas.ubc.ca/instruments/omicron-4-k-stm-afm-with-optical-access-omi/
- updated: 2026-08-19

## Abstract

The Omicron is the LAIR's newest scanning probe microscope: an ultrahigh vacuum, low temperature scanning tunnelling and atomic force microscope with optical access to the tip-sample junction. It has a base temperature around 4 K, with stable imaging at 77 K and room temperature, and a base pressure below 5 x 10-11 mbar. The probe sensor can be either a standard STM tip or a QPlus sensor, the QPlus allowing simultaneous AFM and STM measurement. It is located in an ultra-low vibration space, the Omega pod.

## Systems

NaCl, NbIrTe4, ZnPc, PTCDA, OMBE, SQAO, F8ZnPc, LMC, PtIr, RAM, S3W, ZnPC

LN2/LHe fills continued on a near-weekly cadence throughout the year, several coordinated with Beast/Tesla neighbors (Jisun, James) to monitor cross-talk noise during fills. Throughout February, Vanessa, Jiabin, and Rysa performed iterative quarter- and half-turn adjustments to the M1/M2/M3 and S2/S3 valve screws (logged extensively with extension measurements in mm) chasing persistent stiff, fast-damping east-west motion on the south edge and a clanging sound from M1, eventually ordering brass shim stock (McMaster 9300K29/21) to correct a 0.5-1mm N-S tilt on S2's extension; by Feb 10 the isolators had been relocated ("a new home") and by Feb 14 the block was fully unfloated awaiting shimmed layers, with pendulum-rod alignment (inside the outer housing, undocumented in the manual) still an open question with Steve. ### 2021

Over 2021, the Omicron LT-STM saw extensive electrical noise diagnostics rather than hardware upgrades. In January, Giang mounted NbIrTe4 samples on flag-style Mo/Ta plates and pumped the Prep chamber down to high 10⁻⁷ mbar with the turbo pump, while also arranging cross-lab LEED measurements with Ali and Rysa on the OMBE, checking for the 90° rotation domain seen (or not) in Mohamed's XRD data, using a=0.377 nm, b=1.25 nm lattice constants and ~135 eV beam energy as references from literature on WTe2. A planned repair coordinated through Dan Beaton and Eike Createc stalled for weeks with no fixed date. Starting in late February, Giang undertook a systematic investigation of spiky 60 Hz noise and a persistent forward/backward I(V) offset in the Matrix controller: disconnecting the preamp from the Matrix eliminated the spikes, wrapping the preamp connector pinouts in aluminum foil killed the 60 Hz component, and increasing T-raster from 3 ms to 10–20 ms suppressed the residual spiky noise, pointing to a charging/discharging effect in the preamp capacitance. Giang further found the spike behavior depended non-monotonically on the number of I(V) data points (e.g., present at 300/301 points, absent at 297–299), a puzzle Sarah noted echoed an old unresolved power-of-two hypothesis. Cross-checking with Jisun on the Tesla system showed the same offset, worse without the "Gap Preset" option enabled, and both instruments were confirmed to apply bias on the sample side; Jisun also reported small (2–5 mV) horizontal I(V) offsets possibly tied to drift, and flagged Vernissage software slowdowns after accumulating over 1000 spectra files in one data folder. Separately, Amy borrowed logbooks 12/13 and a flag-style plate with a hole from the vaultway for exfoliation work, and Jiabin designed Au/Ti (10 nm Ti, 60–80 nm Au) e-beam-evaporated void-array patterns (0.5 µm to ~100 µm squares across a 2×2 mm grid) intended for testing exfoliation yield on the Omicron-associated samples.

### 2020

Through 2020 the Omicron STM in AMPEL 47C was run primarily by Giang Nguyen and Jiabin, under Sarah Burke's supervision, with early-year work focused on molecular deposition tests—Giang confirmed on Jan 18 that a higher-temperature deposition of HMAT gave the desired fragment/bond-breaking pattern seen in the new feature images—before the group pivoted to a NbIrTe4 crystal, cleaved for the first time in-situ using the carousel and wobble stick in the STM chamber (rather than the prep chamber) on Jan 22, and imaged first at LN2 and then at LHe temperatures starting Jan 23. A serious cryostat problem emerged after the new He recovery line was connected for the first time post-New Year: the system could only be filled to ~30 mm instead of the normal ~305 mm, traced to water ice likely introduced via back-flow through the new recovery system, and outlet-gas dryness checks using James's desiccant were inconclusive; the fix involved putting the STM in baking position and blowing warm N2 through the inner cryostat at 94°C (variac at 74V) from Feb 5–7, letting it sit near 305 K, then slowly cooling back down, finally reaching 4.5 K on Feb 12 after Jiabin and Giang's efforts. Once stable, the system ran a series of long grid-map QPI scans on NbIrTe4 (18h, 4h, 4.5h, 7h, and a final 22h scan on Feb 26–28 before the APS meeting) mapping step edges, mirror twin boundaries, and grain boundaries, though Matrix software crashed repeatedly on grids over ~24h/high pixel-channel counts, prompting Gary and Erik to recommend splitting files at scan z200–300, disabling unnecessary channels, and rebooting after each fill, with crash recovery via the "agglutinate" tool. Hardware-wise, Giang reset the slab isolator (Specs) pressure from ~120 psi down to 0 and back up to ~110 psi on Mar 2, the pod lighting was slated for electrician repair, and the Omicron-LT preamp was lent out to the Tesla STM group (Jisun/Amy) on Eike's suggestion to diagnose a current problem, with a second, uncertain-condition backup preamp also identified on the shelf. By mid-March, with COVID-19 disruptions beginning, Giang took the lab book, a monitor, and his data home to analyze two months of NbIrTe4 results and begin learning Quantum Espresso for DFT support from Ilya, while Jiabin kept the Omicron running and confirmed the system was accessible remotely via TeamViewer for continued operation.

### 2019

Through 2019 the lab's Omicron LT-STM saw heavy use and several hardware scares: in late January Erik, Giang and Sarah wrestled the microscope's controller down to the basement using two pallet jacks (one of the lab's narrow jacks having permanently vanished), swapped in a loaner controller, and spent a night troubleshooting a dead tunneling current before confirming the loaner wasn't powering the preamp and reverting to the original controller the next day. Gary and Ali etched new silver STM tips (one "new new" tip that gave clean atomic resolution on Ag(111) but no luminescence, requiring re-dipping), prepped Au(111)/mica and Ag(111) crystals, and grew NaCl films of 2-4 ML plus C60, ZnPc, and PTCDA overlayers for a growing STM-induced luminescence (STML) program led by Giang; a blown fuse in early March (traced to the sample arm grounding the HV line during a grab) was replaced, restoring emission current, and a March power spike tripped the interlocks, requiring a manual power-up sequence (matrix controller off, rack on, ion pumps, then ion gauges, then supplies). Liquid-helium fills for both the STM cryostat and CCD dewar were coordinated regularly with Seokhwan's Createc dewar schedule (23-28 L LHe logged in March), and the He line to the dewar was briefly closed for construction work by Dilyar. Giang's spectroscopy push produced PL/EL spectra of ZnPc and PTCDA on NaCl/Ag(111) and NaCl/Au(111), but a software bug was found in March that had been stretching the spectrometer's wavelength axis by 2x, requiring recalibration of all prior STML data (shifting the PTCDA peak to ~675 nm); follow-up tests in April showed the 500 nm blaze/300 g/mm grating gave better signal and range than the 650 nm/600 g/mm grating previously used, that slit widths below 1 mm cut signal without improving resolution, and that Ag(111) surface plasmon signal was roughly 10x stronger than on Au(111), with the lab's Au thin film substrate 40% weaker still. By April the group had also moved on to imaging and DFT of a new self-assembled HMAT molecule on Ag(111), with Gary recording a full bias grid from -2.5 to 2.3 V.

### 2018

Over the course of 2018 the Omicron STM system moved from a persistent electronic noise problem to a productive run of luminescence and gridding experiments. In July, Omicron (the company) proposed a flat-rate repair for the noise issue, with a loaner controller unavailable until October and actual servicing/testing pushed to that month; separately, the lab discovered the Omicron control computer couldn't be added to the automated backup network because of its unsupported OS tied to the Matrix acquisition software. Sarah handled a full system bake and pumpdown in late September, reporting prep-chamber pressure in the high 10⁻⁸ range and LT chamber down to 6e-10, restarting ion pumps and running TSP (prep only) with the Matrix software initially off; Erik subsequently checked walkers, piezos, and sample condition, finding the sample only slightly dirty. Gary ran long STM grids (an ~11:30am finish in late September, a 43-hour grid in November to map energy-level shifts, and another finishing mid-December), and by late October the group achieved tip-induced plasmon emission on Au(111), with gary aligning signal and depositing F8ZnPc on NaCl/Au(111)/mica for luminescence measurements. Through November gary tracked shifts in the gold plasmon peak (from a "textbook" position to an anomalous ~560 nm feature at 3V/300pA), attributing changes in signal-to-noise and peak position to tip-shape evolution and possible adsorbates picked up during tip cleaning/forming. Software stability was also an issue: in November gary hit a Matrix crash that stopped data saving, worked around it by switching save files, and logged a summary in the notebook for troubleshooting. Aaron Kraft's RGA electron-multiplier unit remained on loan to the Omicron setup through October, and Erik and Katherine tracked down banana-to-BNC cabling for the salt evaporator's power supply in September to keep evaporations running.

### 2017

In 2017 the Omicron STM saw a series of tip and junction interventions as the group chased clean spectroscopy: Brandon and Priya swapped in a fresh electrochemically-etched W tip after repeated bias spectroscopy showed a double-gap signature around 4 K, re-annealed the sample stage at 400°C for 20 minutes under UHV (base pressure holding at 2×10⁻¹⁰ mbar) before re-cooling with LHe, and logged a suspicious contamination event on the STM head — the "ewwwwww" moment — traced to a small leak past an old Viton O-ring on the load-lock, prompting a bake-out of the transfer chamber to 120°C overnight and replacement of the seal; by December the tip had been re-sharpened via field emission on a Au(111) reference, restoring atomic resolution and clean dI/dV curves free of the earlier gap artifact.
