# 4probe

- kind: instrument
- channel: CLXDY4AK1
- updated: 2026-08-19

## Abstract

The 4-probe is a four-tip scanning tunnelling microscope under construction by the group, not yet a running instrument. It is built around a Joule-Thomson cryostat with nested radiation shields at 4 K, 20 K, 77 K and 220 K, with Nanonis control replacing the Createc software used elsewhere, and a custom switch box routing TTL signals between the probes.

## Systems

GGS, NDA, CMC, GVs, OCI, NEG, PVA, LED, VPs, POWER, SRS, LS336

### 2026-06

### 2026-05

### 2025

The 4probe spent 2025 moving from design to hardware. Markus iterated extensively on the coax wiring scheme for the JT stage — starting from a custom compact connector (70x12x9.5mm, M2-10 clamping screws), through mini-SMP with a PEEK sleeve/set-screw design that Pinder flagged as fragile, to a double-sided male-male SMP solution with a redesigned M4 clamping collar and hex fastening feature added in March; ten each of mini-SMP connectors were ordered March 19. UHV compatibility of SMP hardware was tested in a spare chamber (vacuum held fine, though not baked above 100°C), and after finding the Accuglass 25-way Kapton/Ag-plated-Cu ribbon cables unsuitable (too thermally conductive between RT and 20K despite being 2kV/600VAC rated), the team pivoted to CF feedthroughs with TE Connectivity 964320-1 receptacle bushes and 964318-1 adapter terminals (still manufactured, though the pistol crimp tool PN 549043-1 is discontinued). Bert's guidance drove a shift to coax-inside-cryo/triax-outside wiring with floating, non-shorted feedthroughs and DC-biased guards to kill parasitic capacitance. OFHC copper 2" round bar was sourced from McMaster for the STM head suspension, machined by Harish in the QMI shop after hours once the CNC/air issue at his other shop killed that plan. On the chamber side, PREVAC finished and shipped the main chamber (cleared customs and delivered by March 27), CryoVac's JT model was finalized with all M2.5 tapped radial holes and 9 CF40 top ports (2×25-pin plus up to 7 coax), and a sputter gun/nipple package was ordered with a 27% CFI discount, fixed at a 110mm (125mm nipple) source-sample distance, stainless-steel (1.4301) shield chosen over copper. A FerroVac manipulator and z-motor controller arrived March 24-27. The He transfer line was redesigned so the KF connection sits ~1550mm above floor to match the Tesla system, with the flexible section bent up 50mm (adjustable +100-150mm) per Sarah's request for fill-level flexibility. Separately, the long-awaited VAT gate valves finally arrived March 28 after nearly a three-month saga involving a mailed cheque in January.

### 2023

Through 2023 the 4-probe remained a system under design and procurement rather than an operating instrument, with Markus Altthaler doing the bulk of the CAD work in SolidWorks. Early in the year Sarah Burke pursued a cryostat quote from Sven at CryoVac, including a sole-source justification modeled on a prior Nanonis purchase, while Markus built a model of the known cryostat parts (LHe stage, JT stage, and magnet, the latter with a 30mm gap height) directly from CryoVac drawings, then iterated on the STM head suspension: a base plate mounted to the JT stage on three rods, a truncated 50mm mating plate, and successive redesigns of the JT heat shield fastening (first a rotate-and-remove scheme, then a shared-flange "teeth" design with 4 bolts for the STM suspension and 4x3 bolts for the heat shield) to work within the 57mm bore at the JT base and the DN400CF flange constraint. By mid-February the group used old CryoVac drawing S01-7900 to rough in preliminary heat shields, yielding a chamber roughly 700mm tall plus flange (heat shields extending 469mm below the flange), later trimmed to a "minimum" main body volume of about 0.063 m³ (a ~0.025 m³ reduction) after discussion of transfer-arm and floor-height constraints. A wobble stick and multi-port flange were added to the model in late March, with the magnet geometry limiting the STM head's view angle to under 7.5° from above the wobble stick, pending more detailed CryoVac drawings to confirm feasibility. In April and May Markus compiled a parts list and began sourcing sample-transport hardware, comparing Omicron's spare-parts catalogue (via contact Eike) against Ferrovac components (including a RECOMSTACK carousel) and a UHV prep manipulator, with Sarah specifying indirect e-beam heating above 1000°C, direct-current heating option, cooling below 100 K, and a 15–30° rotational deadzone; Markus set a default manipulator z-range of 500mm (with a 700mm option) and a transfer-arm travel length of 850mm. On the vacuum side, the group's Pfeiffer Vacuum HiPace300 turbo pump and controller were loaned to the ARPES group in April after a pump malfunction and returned by September. Budget tracking was reorganized under worktags NGDA-S (salaries), NGDA-I (instrument), and NGDA-D (development & assembly), and CryoVac supplied field calculations and magnet dimensions from Joseph, feeding an updated model ahead of a planned May 23–24 meeting between Markus, Joseph, and Bert at Juelich and customer acceptance drawings expected by September 15. A co-op search run by Ken Wong drew 13 applicants, five were interviewed March 15, and Luke Gallant was hired to start May 2 to assist with the CAD effort.

In 2022 the 4probe system existed largely on paper and in CAD as the group worked to reconstruct and redesign its prep chamber and cryostat before any hardware installation: Sarah recovered a corrupted SolidWorks assembly from a Google Drive archive (saved to C:\4-probe-CAD, missing 16 components), and Markus later got SolidWorks running on Brandon Stuart's old lab PC via a local "bsadmin" account, eventually reconstructing a workable model via a Parmeet-generated "pack and go," still missing a DF63FC gate valve model. Through summer, Markus iterated prep-chamber CAD (top/bottom/height-cut slides, LEED port placement, 105° PEEM source/analyzer flange angle carried over from the old design) while the team solicited a LEED-AES quote from OCI (44,270 CAD for 6", 47,430 CAD for 8", no extra mu-metal shield needed if the magnet stays ~1 m away and off during operation). In parallel, Markus and James ran a materials test for a proposed wooden damping frame, baking hard- and soft-wood samples in the box oven (moved to room 145, exhaust line added) at 150°C for four days: soft wood lost 32.2 g (14.3%, 225.4→193.2 g) and hardwood lost 49.6 g (20.4%, 242.8→193.2 g), with charring and a front-to-back color gradient but no burning or significant warping, informing two candidate frame designs (an aluminum triangle copied from the Tesla frame vs. a broken Al-ring/wood hybrid). Design work on the STM head progressed via videoconferences with Bert at Jülich, yielding an updated .stp model with a top-mounted "plug" mating plate on the JT stage (raising heat-load questions for CryoVac) and a 32-cable wiring list; Sarah and Markus began 3D-printing scaled STL exports of the assembly (including a Lego-scale attempt and STL files split into scanner-head and plug sub-assemblies) using the QMI Ultimaker S5 dual-extruder printer, requiring purchase of PVA support material and a special nozzle (~150 CAD). Jisun and Markus also began consolidating scattered invoices (turbo pump protection shields, pump quotes) into a shared OneDrive budget tracker, working through permissions issues with Ken Wong before the file became broadly editable by year's end.

### 2021

Over the course of early 2021 the 4probe team focused heavily on cryostat and STM-head design work coordinated with the Jülich collaborators (Bert and Vasily) and Cryovac's engineers, while also onboarding a new co-op student. Pedro Lopes and Giang Nguyen ran interviews for the co-op position, ultimately hiring Parmeet (with Ved Varshney and Jessie as runners-up) at a stipend of $2,300–2,500/month, following NSERC USRA guidelines; Giang set up a shared Google Drive folder for SolidWorks drawings of the Tesla magnet system to bring the new hire up to speed. On the hardware side, Jisun confirmed the Tesla magnet's bake-out temperature at 120°C (with 100°C as a lower bound), noting the magnets as the limiting component and specifying that two small pumps must run continuously during bake-out. Giang met with Bert and Vasily on the STM head design, flagging that the current head lacks optical access except for a narrow front-view sample-transfer gap, and raised questions about drilling additional viewport holes and their incline angles, as well as confirming head dimensions (referencing mprobes.com/tetraprobe as a design comparison). A significant design concern emerged around magnetic stray field interference with the TEM lab in Vault C: Giang's rough dipole-field estimates put the 4-probe's stray field at ~10 mT versus ~2 mT for the Tesla at 0.5 m, but after accounting for the roughly 13.8 m distance to Vault C, Cryovac's simulation showed only ~µT-level fields; after speaking with Alan Maigne (TEM contact), Giang learned the actual requirement is that the DC field change during ramping stay below 30 nT, and the team discussed mu-metal sheet shielding around the chamber as a backup mitigation if needed, though ramp rates were deemed slow enough to avoid trouble. Jisun also examined the 1 K shield design, confirming it is open at the bottom for a switching mechanism but substantially obstructed by an eddy-current damping component. Meetings with the Cryovac engineer culminated in Giang producing an initial cryostat sketch on March 11 for team feedback as the design phase moved forward.
